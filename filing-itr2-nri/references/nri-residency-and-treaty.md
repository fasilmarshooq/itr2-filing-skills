# NRI residency and treaty evidence

## Ask in this order

Ask one focused question at a time and reuse travel or tax documents already
provided.

### 1. Physical presence

Build an entry/exit calendar and ask for:

- total days in India during the **current financial year**,
- total days in India during each of the **four preceding** financial years,
- additional seven-year/ten-year history if required to distinguish
  ROR/RNOR/NR under current law,
- Indian citizenship/PIO status and the purpose of relevant visits,
- Indian-source income needed for any special threshold test.

Use arrival/departure dates consistently and show the count to the user. Verify
the current Section 6 rules, citizen/PIO exceptions, deemed-resident rule, and
portal status options from official sources. “Within 180 days” is not a legal
conclusion.

### 2. Foreign tax residence

Ask for:

- country or **tax jurisdiction** of residence,
- **Taxpayer Identification Number** or the jurisdiction's functional
  equivalent,
- tax-residency period covered,
- whether a **TRC** (Tax Residency Certificate) has been obtained,
- whether local tax was filed/paid where relevant.

A TRC is normally issued or certified by the foreign tax authority under that
country's process; it is not automatically the foreign tax return itself.
Timelines vary, so identify the need early.

### 3. Indian-source inventory

Reconcile:

- Indian salary or services,
- rent/house property,
- bank interest by NRE/NRO/other account type,
- dividends from Indian companies/funds,
- Indian securities and mutual-fund gains,
- property transactions,
- pension and other receipts,
- TDS/TCS and taxes paid.

Indian dividend remains Indian-source even when retained in India. Whether
money was repatriated abroad does not by itself change Indian taxability.
Confirm any claimed NRE-interest exemption from account type and current law;
do not generalize it to NRO interest.

### 4. Treaty decision

For each item where treaty treatment could improve the domestic result:

1. Compute the domestic-law treatment first.
2. Read the official treaty and protocol for the actual jurisdiction.
3. Identify the article, rate/relief mechanism, beneficial-ownership or other
   conditions, and documentation deadline.
4. Confirm TRC and Form 10F/other evidence required under current Indian rules.
5. Compare the supported treaty outcome with domestic law.

Do not claim a DTAA rate or exemption without official treaty support and
evidence. If the TRC has not been obtained, mark the treaty position
`UNSUPPORTED/PENDING`; do not advise claiming now and producing proof only if
the department asks later.

### 5. FPI distinction

Ask whether the person is an **FPI** only when the portal requires it. FPI means
a registered Foreign Portfolio Investor; ordinary NRI ownership of an Indian
broker/demat account does not make the person an FPI. Verify registration
evidence before selecting Yes.

## Output

Add this concise block to the filing ledger:

```text
Residential status: NR/RNOR/ROR
Basis: day counts + rule verified on DATE from OFFICIAL URL
Foreign jurisdiction/TIN: verified or missing
TRC/Form 10F: obtained / pending / not required, with reason
Treaty claims: income item → domestic result → treaty result → evidence
Open risk: none or explicit escalation
```
