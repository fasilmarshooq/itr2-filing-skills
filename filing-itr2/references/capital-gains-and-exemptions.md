# Capital gains and exemptions

## Classify before entering

Use the current AY's official instructions and schema. Build a separate row for
each legally distinct bucket before aggregating.

| Evidence | Likely bucket to verify |
|---|---|
| Listed equity/equity-oriented fund with STT, short-term | Section 111A |
| Listed equity/equity-oriented fund with STT, long-term | Section 112A and Schedule 112A |
| Debt/non-equity mutual fund | Acquisition-date and fund-type rules, including Section 50AA where applicable |
| Property/land | Property CG fields and stamp-duty-value rules |
| Unlisted company shares | Unquoted-share fields and valuation evidence |
| Gold, bond, ETF, other capital asset | “Other assets” bucket after verifying the specific asset |

### Hard classification guard

Do not place mutual fund, ETF, quoted security, or ordinary debt-fund proceeds
in the portal's **unquoted shares** fields merely because the generic section
contains mandatory inputs. Use unquoted-share fields only for actual unquoted
company shares. For assets other than unquoted shares, use the corresponding
“assets other than unquoted shares” consideration field.

Do not aggregate:

- 111A gains/losses with slab-rate gains,
- 112A rows that require scrip-wise reporting with unrelated assets,
- pre/post rule-change acquisitions without confirming identical treatment, or
- sale consideration with gain.

Reconcile consideration, cost, permitted transfer expenditure, disallowed loss,
holding period, and sale-date quarter. Verify whether each charge is deductible;
never assume all broker charges are.

## Section 54F workflow

Treat 54F as an eligibility and chronology decision, not a balancing entry.
Section 54F concerns a gain from an original long-term capital asset **other than
a residential house**. It is not available for short-term capital gain. A gain
from transferring a long-term residential house may instead require a Section
54 analysis. Verify the applicable section and current official conditions
before collecting an exemption amount.

Verify the current official rule and collect:

1. Original long-term asset type and each transfer date.
2. Net consideration, cost, transfer expenses, and computed LTCG.
3. New residential house location, purchase/construction status, registration
   or completion/possession date, price, and payment evidence.
4. Residential houses owned on each relevant transfer date.
5. Other house purchases/construction within the restricted period.
6. Amount invested by the return due date and any Capital Gains Accounts Scheme
   deposit, date, account, and IFSC.

Check chronology separately for every original transfer. The new-house purchase
may precede a transfer only within the officially permitted window. A registration
date is evidence of purchase, not automatically the only legally relevant date.
Do not substitute the new-house registration or purchase date for the original
asset transfer date; record both from their respective evidence.

Compute the maximum deduction using the current Section 54F formula. Full LTCG
is not automatically exempt merely because the house costs more than the gain:
the investment is tested against **net consideration**, subject to the statutory
cap and all eligibility conditions. Claim no more than the supported LTCG.

Enter the same deduction consistently in:

- the relevant LTCG sub-item,
- Schedule CG's deduction-information block, and
- the resulting set-off/tax schedules.

If ownership, chronology, construction status, net consideration, or treaty
interaction is uncertain, stop and recommend professional review before claiming.
